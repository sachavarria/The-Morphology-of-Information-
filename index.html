00 / Origin of inquiry

From an image
to an experiment.

In November 2026, a widely circulated visualization of a reported AI-assisted formalization of the Riemann hypothesis brought to mind the organization of an animal nervous system. Its central axis was deliberately emphasized, prompting a question about the relation between computational topology and visual form.

Could the resemblance reveal common organizational principles, or did it arise from the spatial translation of the data? The encounter became a series of computational experiments. The results, in turn, changed how the image could be perceived.

Point of departure: Takens Theorem, 22 November 2026. The original artwork is referenced, not reproduced. This atlas investigates other formal mathematical datasets; the exact graph used in that visualization has not been recovered.

01 / Morphologies

Keep the relations.

When its data are available, each empirical graph is paired with a randomized network retaining the same node identities, in-degrees and out-degrees, and weak connectedness. Transform their spatial organization and observe what persists.

Mathematics is included. Biological and geographic samples are omitted from the public distribution; local research bundles can be opened without uploading data to a server.

A physical form; an abstract topology.

In the regional graph, each reach connects to at most one downstream successor. There are 965,037 edges and no directed cycles. The result is a directed forest; flows converge but cannot split in this encoding. Real rivers can bifurcate, and the representation does not capture every hydrological process.

For a complete 330-reach basin, selected by closest size to the other atlas samples (ties broken by the smallest outlet ID), we generated 500 randomized trees with the exact same degrees and outlet. Its depth of 35 connections lies within the randomized central 95% interval of 26–58 (median 38).

The null model uses uniformly shuffled multiset Prüfer sequences and orients the resulting labeled trees toward the original outlet. It preserves the entire in/out-degree sequence, but ignores geography, river lengths, slope and flow. The result describes this one basin, not all river systems.

03 / Experiments

A structure that survives the image.

Feedforward motif

A relation connects A to C, both directly and through B. This small topological structure recurs across the mathematical, bacterial and neuronal graphs. The biological function and mathematical meaning of the relation are different. The pattern itself can be measured independently of any spatial representation.

The strictly induced motif excludes any additional connections among these three nodes. In the acyclic graphs, this is equivalent to the ordered three-edge pattern.

Full networkObservedControl medianEnrichment

Controls are specific to each system: mathematical degrees, exact hierarchy and source-directory categories; bacterial degrees and hierarchy; neural degrees and reciprocity. All self-loops are excluded. The finite control ensembles are exploratory; the ratios are not measures of relative complexity. For the river forest, 0/0 is undefined.

What survives the removal of a relation?

Transitive reduction preserves all directed reachability in an acyclic graph. It removes 41,664 of 304,433 recorded mathematical relations and 42 of 519 non-self bacterial relations. The river graph is already a forest and admits no such removals.

What is indispensable?

Reachability redundancy is a graph property. A direct mathematical dependency can be necessary to the actual proof; a direct regulatory interaction can be essential to the organism. The operation says nothing by itself about functional or logical dispensability.

04 / Research notes

Knowledge changes the conditions of perception.

The initial aesthetic encounter produced a research question. The resulting experiments changed what could be perceived in the original encounter. This recursive relation between aesthetic experience and knowledge is the conceptual structure of the atlas, within the ongoing research on cognitive aesthetics.

Experiment 001

Resemblance

Two small empirical networks and randomized controls. Geometry and connectivity become separate objects of inquiry.

Experiment 002

Density

Network bottlenecks change when connectivity is matched. A second curated mathematical graph extends the pilot.

Experiment 003

Motifs

Mathlib, bacterial transcription and the connectome reveal repeated three-node relations.

Experiment 004

Invariance

Hierarchy, subject-directory and reciprocity constraints alter the null expectation; induced motifs remain enriched under tailored controls.

Experiment 005

Convergence

A continental river forest shares branching morphology while excluding feedforward relations by construction.

What remains unresolved

The mathematical result still needs independent extraction and cross-language replication (e.g. Metamath). The exact graph underlying the original viral image has not been recovered. The randomized ensembles from prior experiments need more rigorous sampling validation. Novelty is not established.

Four different relations

Logical use, transcriptional regulation, chemical connectivity and downstream drainage cannot be interpreted as equivalent physical operations. The atlas compares specified graph properties and explores the consequences of their representation.

05 / Practice and method

Research in conversation
with synthetic intelligence.

S.A. Chavarría / Anti-disciplinary artist, writer, researcher and professor

As an anti-disciplinary artist, writer, researcher and professor, my practice encompasses computational language, mathematics, synthetic intelligence and the study of cognition. I am particularly interested in the possibilities that emerge through sustained intellectual collaboration between human and synthetic intelligences.

Alongside The Morphology of Information, I am currently pursuing a mathematical investigation into an unresolved problem concerning the reconstruction of binary sequences from the compositions of their substrings. This collaboration has produced a proposed proof that would resolve a gap between previously established mathematical bounds. The result remains subject to independent mathematical scrutiny, assessment of its originality and formal verification.

These investigations address distinct questions within my broader research into cognitive aesthetics, mathematical discovery and synthetic intelligence. I am interested in what we can discover together, how our respective capacities participate in the production of knowledge, and how my own capacity for understanding evolves through this relationship.

The Morphology of Information is itself an artifact of this research methodology, made possible through sustained conversation with artificial intelligence. Its production also raises questions of epistemic responsibility, particularly concerning the verification, interpretation and communication of collaboratively produced knowledge.

The collaborative methodology of this project emerges from a sustained relationship with artificial intelligence developed over years, across different models, technological architectures and artistic investigations. This history informs how I formulate questions, challenge interpretations, recognize possibilities and develop ideas through conversation.

The direction of the inquiry evolves through an iterative process of proposition, debate, rejection, experimentation and revision. AI participates in the formulation of hypotheses, computational implementation, analysis and visualization, extending the scale and kinds of investigation I am able to undertake.

I am interested in how this relationship expands my intellectual and artistic capacities, and how the process of collaboratively producing knowledge transforms my own understanding.

The experimental findings remain exploratory and open to independent verification.
